C

Part C · Build the architecture

About 20 minutes · a warm-up with feedback · 6 auto-marked questions

An architecture (البنية المعمارية للنظام) is a map of the parts of a service and the data that flows between them. Health IoT services have four layers: perception (الاستشعار), where devices measure; network (الشبكة), where a gateway (بوابة) and a connection carry the data; platform (المنصة), where data is stored and rules run; and application (التطبيقات), where people see the data and act. Governance wraps all four. Tap each layer, then step through a reading’s journey.

Tip. When a service fails, find the layer where it failed. Most failures happen at the joins: a device not paired, an alert nobody sees, a note never written back.

The layers of a health IoT service

Tap a layer to see what it does, what sits in it, and what usually fails there.

Case 1 · Nights at a hospital in Ma’an

Hospital · Models 2 and 4

A 100-bed Ministry of Health hospital in Ma’an, more than 200 km south of Amman, has pharmacists on site only from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. At night and at weekends, nurses take medicines from ward stock without any pharmacist check. Last month a patient with poor kidney function received a full-dose enoxaparin order at night and developed a serious bleed. Nurses on the medical ward also prepare intravenous antibiotics themselves, and nobody checks the preparation.

The hospital uses Hakeem for records and computerised order entry (إدخال الأوامر الدوائية المحوسب). The Ministry has agreed that pharmacists at a large teaching hospital in Amman can support the hospital remotely. The internet link is usually good but fails for an hour or two a few times each month.

The hospital director asks you to design a night and weekend telepharmacy service.

  • 31Choose the model or models. Name the core model and one supporting model for this hospital, and justify each with one fact from the case.
  • 32Describe the architecture. List the components at the hospital, the connection, and the hub in Amman, and describe (or draw) how one night-time order moves from entry to administration.
  • 33Triage and escalation. Which orders should the remote pharmacist review first, what patient data must they be able to see, and what happens when a medicine is needed before review is possible?
  • 34Risks and safeguards. Identify one technical risk and one clinical or legal risk, and give a practical safeguard for each.
  • 35Measures. Propose three indicators to judge the service after six months (one process, one safety and one outcome indicator), each with a target.

Case 2 · Abu Salem at home in the Jordan Valley

Home · telecare, telemonitoring, IoT and Model 3

Abu Salem, 72, lives with his wife in Deir Alla in the Jordan Valley, where summer temperatures pass 40 °C. He has heart failure, hypertension and type 2 diabetes treated with insulin, and takes nine medicines. He was admitted twice this year with fluid overload (احتباس السوائل). His daughter Maha is his main carer, but she works in Salt three days a week, and on those days he often misses his evening doses. His wife is unsteady on her feet and fell last winter.

The nearest pharmacy is 15 km away. Abu Salem has a basic smartphone and Maha has a smartphone. The family fridge is old and the kitchen becomes very hot in summer; his insulin is stored in the fridge door.

A community pharmacy group in Salt, working with the physician at the local health centre, wants to offer him an IoT-supported service.

  • 36Classify the needs. From Abu Salem’s situation, give one example that needs telecare, one that needs telemonitoring, and one that needs telepharmacy (Model 3).
  • 37Choose two IoT devices. For each, state what it senses, who receives the data, and what action follows. One device must protect his insulin from the summer heat.
  • 38Write one traffic-light rule for his daily weight or his blood pressure: define green, amber and red, who responds, and how fast.
  • 39Privacy and consent. Who should see what (Maha, the pharmacist, the physician), how is consent recorded, and which Jordanian law governs his health data?
  • 40Adoption and sustainability. Name one barrier to Abu Salem using the service and a design change that removes it, and one factor that decides whether the service can continue after its first year.

Glossary

Digital healthالصحة الرقمية
The broadest term for using digital technology in health. It includes eHealth, mHealth, wearables, artificial intelligence and health data science.
eHealthالصحة الإلكترونية
The use of information and communication technology for health.
Telehealthالصحة عن بُعد
Health services and information delivered at a distance. It covers clinical care, and also education, training and administration.
Telemedicineالطب عن بُعد
Clinical care, such as assessment, diagnosis, treatment and follow-up, delivered at a distance.
Telepharmacyالصيدلة عن بُعد
Pharmaceutical care provided by a licensed pharmacist to patients at a distance, through telecommunication technology.
mHealthالصحة المتنقلة
Health practice supported by mobile devices such as phones, wearables and wireless monitors.
Telemonitoringالمراقبة الصحية عن بُعد
Also called remote patient monitoring. Body measurements taken outside the clinic are sent to clinicians who review them and act.
Telecareالرعاية عن بُعد
Sensors and alarms at home that support safety and independent living. A response centre, carer or family member answers them.
Internet of Things (IoT)إنترنت الأشياء
Everyday objects fitted with sensors, software and a network connection, which collect and share data without anyone pressing send.
Internet of Medical Things (IoMT)إنترنت الأشياء الطبية
Connected medical devices that are linked to health information systems.
Synchronousمتزامن
Both people take part at the same time, by live video or by phone.
Asynchronous (store-and-forward)غير متزامن (التخزين ثم الإرسال)
Information is captured, stored and reviewed later, for example a scanned prescription in a queue.
Hub and spokeنموذج المحور والأطراف
A central site (the hub) with pharmacists supports several remote sites (the spokes).
Remote dispensingالصرف عن بُعد
Model 1. A remote site releases medicines to patients only after a pharmacist has verified them from a distance.
Remote order reviewمراجعة الأوامر الدوائية عن بُعد
Model 2. A pharmacist verifies hospital medication orders from a distance before they are given.
Remote counselling and clinical careالإرشاد الدوائي والرعاية السريرية عن بُعد
Model 3. Counselling, medication review and follow-up by a pharmacist, by video or phone.
Remote supervision of preparation and automationالإشراف عن بُعد على التحضير والأنظمة الآلية
Model 4. A pharmacist checks preparations, such as intravenous doses, or automated equipment through cameras, barcodes and records.
Verificationالتحقق
The pharmacist’s check that the right medicine, dose and label reach the right patient.
Compoundingتحضير الأدوية وتركيبها
Preparing a medicine for a specific patient, for example an intravenous infusion.
Automated dispensing cabinetخزانة صرف آلية
A locked, computer-controlled cabinet that stores medicines and releases them to authorised users.
Teach-backالتحقق بالإعادة
The patient explains the plan in their own words, or shows the technique, so you know they understood.
Escalationالتصعيد
Moving a patient to urgent or in-person care when a finding is beyond what remote care can safely handle.
Red flagعلامة خطر
A finding that needs urgent action.
Thresholdحدّ التنبيه
The value at which a reading triggers an alert.
Alert fatigueإرهاق التنبيهات
When so many alerts arrive that staff begin to ignore them, including the important ones.
Consentالموافقة
The patient’s clear, informed agreement to the service and to how their data will be used.
Confidentialityالسرية
Keeping patient information private and sharing it only with people entitled to see it.
Encryptionالتشفير
Scrambling data so that only authorised people can read it, while it travels (in transit) and while it is stored (at rest).
Audit trailسجل التدقيق
A record of who viewed or changed information, and when.
Role-based accessصلاحيات الوصول حسب الدور
Each user can see and do only what their role needs.
Multi-factor authenticationالمصادقة متعددة العوامل
Logging in with two or more proofs of identity, such as a password and a code sent to a phone.
Interoperabilityالتشغيل البيني
The ability of different systems to exchange data and use it correctly.
Write-backالتوثيق في السجل الصحي المشترك
Sending the encounter note back into the patient’s shared record so that other clinicians can see it.
Gatewayبوابة
A device, often a phone or a home hub, that receives data from sensors and forwards it to the platform.
Platformمنصة
The cloud or server layer that stores data and runs the alert rules.
Dashboardلوحة المتابعة
The screen where clinicians see readings, trends and alerts.
Cold chainسلسلة التبريد
Keeping temperature-sensitive medicines, such as insulin and vaccines, within their safe range from supplier to patient.
Validated deviceجهاز مثبتة دقته
A measuring device whose accuracy has been tested against a recognised protocol.
Adherenceالالتزام بالعلاج
Taking medicines as agreed with the prescriber.
Key performance indicator (KPI)مؤشر أداء رئيسي
A measure used to judge whether a service is working.
Hakeemحكيم
Jordan’s national electronic health record programme.
Sensitive personal dataبيانات شخصية حساسة
Types of data, including health data, that need extra protection under Jordan’s Personal Data Protection Law No. 24 of 2023.
